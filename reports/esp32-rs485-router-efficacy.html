Executive verdict

Make it frame-aware and topology-aware. Stop before it becomes a full motion proxy.

Smart filtering creates a large reliability gain inside each branch, but it cannot increase the shared outer bus capacity. The immediate performance bottleneck is the Router’s fixed broadcast pacing—not the lack of an aggregate move protocol.

03 · Two-stage protocols

Aggregate feedback, not movement

Movement payload dominates its frame; grouping saves little. Feedback polling contains many request/reply turnarounds and can exploit six inner buses operating in parallel.

Existing

Keyframe movement

  • Nine positions and velocities per aligned batch
  • No per-Portal ACK traffic
  • Stale frames already collated
  • Approximately 225–230 B per group

Keep and optimize

Low return

Repeater-owned group move

  • Carries essentially the same motion values
  • Likely under 10% byte reduction
  • Adds proxy ACK and partial-failure semantics
  • Does not solve cross-branch timing by itself

Do not implement

Conditional opportunity

Aggregate position snapshot

  • Six branches collect nine replies in parallel
  • Six outer replies instead of 54
  • Predicted 130–250 ms sweep
  • Approximately 2–4× faster than direct polling

Add only when feedback demand justifies it

Synchronization is a different problem

A group protocol does not make six sequential outer packets arrive simultaneously. With current batches, the last branch can receive its update roughly 100–120 ms after the first. If tighter visual synchronization is required, timestamped future execution is more effective: transmit all six frames early and apply them against a shared future time.

04 · Inner baud

Useful headroom, unchanged ceiling

The ESP32 UARTs may use different baud rates. The WaveShare-facing side stays at its documented 115200 while the isolated Portal-facing side can be bench-tested at higher rates.

06 · Boundaries

Assumptions and confidence

Values are engineering predictions derived from wire format and baud-rate airtime. They should be confirmed with captures from the production WaveShare, cable plant, ESP32 board, and Portal firmware.

Six repeaters and 54 globally unique Portals share each outer RS485 channel.
Each repeater’s nine Portal IDs form a discoverable contiguous range.
Position+velocity keyframes are the performance-critical traffic.
RS485 uses 115200, 8 data bits, no parity, and one stop bit.
Nine-entry worst-case keyframes occupy approximately 225–230 wire bytes.
The first production router keeps both UARTs at 115200.
What should be measured on the bench?

Outer and inner byte counts; frame loss; CRC/COBS errors; ESP32 UART overruns; WaveShare TCP-to-UART backlog; queue high-water marks; first-to-last branch latency; direction-control timing; and full-system update completion at each candidate broadcast gap.

Why not simply try an unsupported WaveShare baud?

The manufacturer specifies 300–115200 for this model. Even if an undocumented setting worked, it would create an unsupported dependency in the shared bottleneck. A faster inner UART is isolated from the WaveShare and therefore the safer experiment.

When would a more complicated router become justified?

When position/status feedback must run frequently during motion, when timestamped synchronized execution is required, or when the repeater must autonomously continue motion during loss of the PC connection. Those are additional product capabilities, not simple bandwidth optimizations.
